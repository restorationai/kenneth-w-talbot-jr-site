**Burst pipe in your Freeport home?** We answer 24/7, call (337) 344-1248 now. A supply line failure can put hundreds of gallons into a house in under an hour, and the difference between a contained repair and a gutted floor often comes down to how fast the water gets shut off and extracted.

A burst pipe rarely announces itself politely. It's a bulge in the ceiling drywall that gives way, a sudden drop in water pressure while the dishwasher runs, or the sound of water hammering inside a wall cavity at 2 a.m. In homes built on slab foundations, which make up a large share of the housing stock around Walton County, the break is often invisible until water is already tracking under baseboards or pooling at a low point in the slab. By the time you see a stain, the saturation footprint under the flooring is usually larger than it looks from the surface.

## What burst pipe cleanup and repair actually involves

This service covers two distinct problems that have to be solved together: stopping and repairing the physical pipe failure, and removing the water that already escaped before it causes secondary damage. The plumbing side means isolating the broken section, cutting out failed copper, galvanized, or PEX, and reconnecting with code-compliant fittings. The water damage side means extraction, moisture mapping, and structural drying, because water that migrated into wall cavities, subfloor, or insulation won't dry on its own in Florida's humidity. A straightforward single-room break is often resolved in a few days. A break that ran unnoticed overnight behind a wall or under cabinetry can take longer, since hidden cavities need to be opened, dried, and monitored before they're closed back up.

## Our process

1. **Locate and isolate the break.** We confirm where the pipe failed, shut off the affected line (or the main if the break is upstream of a shutoff), and stop active water flow before anything else happens.
2. **Extract standing water and assess the spread.** Pumps and extraction equipment remove visible water first, then we use moisture meters to trace how far the water traveled under flooring, behind baseboards, and inside wall cavities.
3. **Open and dry affected cavities.** Wet drywall, insulation, and subfloor are removed or dried in place depending on material and saturation level, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed to pull moisture out of the structure, not just the surface.
4. **Repair or replace the failed pipe section.** The damaged segment is cut out and repaired to current plumbing code, with pressure testing before the line goes back into service.
5. **Monitor moisture to dry standard and close out.** We track drying progress with daily or periodic moisture readings until materials read dry, not just dry to the touch, before any rebuild work starts.

## What separates a good burst pipe response from a bad one

The most common mistake is stopping at "the water's gone" instead of confirming the structure is actually dry. Carpet pad and subfloor can look fine on top while holding moisture underneath for days, and closing up a wall before the stud and insulation reach dry standard is how a pipe break turns into a mold problem weeks later. A second mistake is repairing the visible pipe without checking why it failed in the first place. Galvanized and older copper lines often fail because of pinhole corrosion that's happening elsewhere in the same run, and patching one spot without inspecting the rest just delays the next emergency call.

Adjusters reviewing a burst pipe claim typically want documented moisture readings over time, photos of the affected materials before removal, and a clear line between what failed (the pipe) and what it damaged (flooring, drywall, cabinetry). Because our license covers mold assessment and remediation in Florida, we're also positioned to flag and address any secondary mold risk that shows up during drying rather than treating it as a separate, later problem.

Freeport doesn't see hard freezes often, but the occasional cold snap that drops pipe temperatures below freezing catches a lot of homes off guard, since plumbing here isn't typically insulated the way it is further north.

## What do I do in the first 60 seconds?

Shut off the water at the main valve, then kill power to any outlet or fixture the water is touching. Those two actions stop the damage from getting worse while you figure out next steps.

The main shutoff is usually near the street-side meter, in a garage, or under a utility sink. If you don't know where yours is, this is the moment to find it, not the first time you need it. If water is anywhere near an outlet, light switch, or electrical panel, flip the breaker for that area at the panel rather than reaching into standing water.

After the water and power are handled, move anything you can lift off the wet floor, rugs, boxes, furniture legs, and take a few photos on your phone. You'll want those for the insurance claim later, and you don't need to do anything else before making the next two calls.

## Who do I call first, a plumber or a restoration company?

Call both, and call them close together, because they do two different jobs. A licensed plumber stops the leak at the pipe. A water damage restoration company removes the standing water and dries the structure before mold has a chance to start.

A plumber's job ends once the pipe is repaired. They aren't equipped to extract water from under flooring, pull wet baseboard, or run the industrial dehumidifiers and air movers that actually dry out a house. That's the restoration side, and it needs to start the same day, not whenever the plumber happens to have a repair slot open.

## What happens in the first 24 hours after a burst pipe?

The first 24 hours are about getting the water out and the air moving, because wet drywall and wood framing can start growing mold within 24 to 48 hours ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home)).

A restoration crew typically starts with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted or portable pumps, then checks how far the water traveled using a moisture meter on walls, baseboards, and subfloor. Water from a clean supply line starts as Category 1 water, but it doesn't stay clean once it sits on drywall, carpet pad, or framing for more than a few hours, so speed matters.

Any baseboard, drywall, or insulation that's saturated past the point of drying in place usually comes out, rather than being dried in place, to stop it from becoming a hidden mold source. Air movers and dehumidifiers then run for several days, with daily moisture checks until the readings return to normal for the material.

## Will homeowners insurance cover a burst pipe in Florida?

Most Florida homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage from a burst pipe, but they generally exclude damage from a leak that was gradual or went unaddressed for a long time ([Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance)).

The plumbing repair itself may or may not be covered depending on your policy's wording, but the water damage cleanup, drying, and any removed materials usually fall under the dwelling or contents coverage. Document everything: photos before anything is moved, the plumber's invoice, and a written drying scope from the restoration company. Our [guide to homeowners insurance and water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/) covers what adjusters typically ask for.

You also have a duty to mitigate further damage once the leak is stopped, which is part of why insurers expect drying to start quickly rather than days later.

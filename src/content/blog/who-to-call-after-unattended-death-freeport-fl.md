## Who Do You Call First, Before Anyone Calls a Cleanup Crew?

Call 911 immediately. In Freeport, that call routes to the Walton County Sheriff's Office, and deputies will respond to secure the scene before anyone, including family, re-enters the room.

Do not touch anything, open windows, or try to clean while you wait. Deputies need the scene undisturbed to confirm there's no sign of foul play, and disturbing it can complicate both the investigation and any later insurance claim. If the person lived alone and wasn't found for some time, it's normal for law enforcement to take longer than a typical welfare check. That's expected, not a red flag.

## What Happens After You Call 911?

After deputies secure the scene, the medical examiner's office for the district that covers Walton County determines cause and manner of death and authorizes the body's removal. This step can take anywhere from a few hours to a full day depending on the circumstances and current caseload.

Once the medical examiner's office finishes, the scene is formally released back to the family or the property owner. That release is the legal green light for anyone, including a cleanup crew, to enter and begin work. Families typically coordinate with a funeral home at this same point to arrange transport and services; the funeral home's role is separate from, and happens alongside, the cleanup decision.

## When Should You Call an Unattended Death Cleanup Company?

Call a cleanup company as soon as the scene is released, not before. Entering earlier can interfere with the investigation, and entering later gives decomposition fluids more time to soak into subflooring, drywall, and framing, which turns a contained job into a bigger one.

This isn't work for a standard cleaning service. Decomposition fluids are classified as a biohazard under the [OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens standard](https://www.osha.gov/bloodborne-pathogens), which sets specific rules for PPE, handling, and disposal that a general housecleaner isn't equipped or trained to follow. A team that does this work regularly will assess which materials, carpet, subfloor, drywall, sometimes framing, need to be removed versus treated, and will know how far odor and staining have actually traveled beneath the visible surface.

## Why Does This Need Specialized Cleaning Instead of a Deep Clean?

Unattended death cleanup needs specialized handling because decomposition affects more than what's visible on the surface. Fluids wick into porous materials, and the bacteria responsible for odor can persist in subflooring long after a surface wipe-down looks clean.

When you're vetting any company for this kind of job, ask two things directly: do they carry OSHA bloodborne pathogen training, and are they registered with the Florida Department of Health as a provider authorized to transport trauma-scene waste. Florida regulates who can legally remove and transport this type of biomedical waste, so it's a fair question to ask before anyone sets foot in the home. ## Does Homeowners or Renters Insurance Cover This Cleanup?

Some homeowners and renters policies cover unattended death cleanup as part of a covered loss, but coverage isn't automatic and varies by insurer and policy language. Standard policies are built around sudden, accidental events, and an insurer may treat a death cleanup differently depending on the circumstances.

The [Insurance Information Institute's overview of what's covered by standard homeowners insurance](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) is a useful starting point for understanding how insurers generally categorize this kind of claim. The most practical step is to call your agent before work begins, and ask the cleanup company for an itemized scope and photos you can submit with the claim. Keep every receipt; documentation is what turns a maybe into a paid claim.

## What Should Landlords and Property Managers Do After a Tenant Dies Alone in a Rental?

Landlords should secure the unit, avoid entering or cleaning it themselves, and wait for the scene to be released before bringing in a professional crew. Entering early, even to check on the unit, can create liability and interfere with the investigation.

Once released, treat the cleanup the same way you'd treat any serious property loss: get a written scope before work starts, keep every document for insurance and for the unit's maintenance file, and loop in your attorney about any state notification or disclosure obligations before re-listing the unit. Those rules vary and aren't something to guess at.

## Who Should I Call First for Mold Remediation in Freeport, FL?

Call Veterans Remediation & Restoration at (337) 344-1248 first if you can see visible mold growth larger than about a square foot, smell a persistent musty odor, or had recent water intrusion from a leak, storm, or AC condensation issue. Florida treats mold work differently than most states. The Department of Business and Professional Regulation licenses mold assessors and mold remediators as separate roles, and for jobs over 10 square feet, the law generally keeps assessment and remediation work independent to avoid a conflict of interest. ## When Is It a Mold Company Call vs a Plumber or Your Insurer?

Call a plumber first only if water is actively running right now and you can't find the shutoff; stopping an active leak takes priority over everything else because mold can't be remediated while the source keeps feeding it. Once the water is off, the mold conversation starts.

Here's the rough order for most Freeport situations:

- **Water is actively flowing** (burst supply line, overflowing fixture): shut off the water at the valve or the main, then call a restoration company. Don't wait for a scheduled plumber visit if the area is already soaked.
- **Visible mold with no active leak**: call for a mold assessment first. This is the most common Freeport scenario, especially in homes that had minor water intrusion during last year's rainy season that nobody fully dried out.
- **Electrical hazard near the wet or moldy area** (outlets underwater, exposed wiring): cut power at the breaker and call an electrician before anyone goes near the space.
- **Insurance company**: call after a licensed mold assessor has documented the extent of growth and the likely moisture source. Carriers want dated photos, a written scope, and often a separate assessment report before they'll discuss coverage, and mold-related claims in Florida get scrutinized closely.

If you're not sure which category you're in, describing what you see on the phone is usually enough for a dispatcher to tell you what to do next. You don't need to self-diagnose before you call.

## What Happens When You Call for Mold Remediation?

The first call is mostly information gathering: how big the affected area looks, whether there's an active or past water source, what room it's in, and whether anyone in the household has respiratory sensitivities. Have your address, a rough size estimate (a few square inches vs several square feet), and any recent water history ready.

On arrival, a licensed assessor typically checks the extent of visible growth, uses a moisture meter to map how far dampness has traveled behind the surface, and may recommend air or surface sampling if the growth is hidden or the homeowner needs lab-confirmed documentation for an insurance claim or a real estate transaction. From there, remediation involves containment with poly sheeting, negative air pressure with a HEPA air scrubber, removal of unsalvageable porous material like drywall or insulation, and a post-remediation verification step before the area is cleared to rebuild.

The EPA's [guidance on mold remediation](https://www.epa.gov/mold) recommends professional remediation for any contiguous moldy area larger than about 10 square feet, which lines up with Florida's own licensing threshold. ## What If I Just Want to Handle It Myself First?

A small, isolated patch under a square foot on a non-porous surface, like tile or a window sill, is reasonable to clean yourself with detergent and water, according to [IICRC](https://www.iicrc.org) guidance for minor mold conditions. Anything larger, anything on drywall or wood framing, or anything with a recurring musty smell is past the DIY stage.

The risk with DIY on a larger area isn't just that the mold comes back. Disturbing drywall or insulation without containment can spread spores through the HVAC system and turn a one-room problem into a whole-house one. If you've already tried cleaning a spot and it keeps returning within a few weeks, that's usually a sign moisture is still getting into the wall cavity and a licensed assessment is worth the call.

## How do you verify a Florida restoration contractor's license?

You verify a Florida contractor or specialty license by searching the holder's name on the Florida DBPR's [license lookup tool](https://www.myfloridalicense.com/wl11.asp), which shows license status, type, and any disciplinary history.

Do this before any contract is signed, not after. Florida doesn't require a single dedicated "fire restoration license," but if the job involves mold assessment or remediation, which is common once suppression water sits for more than a day, the company performing that specific work does need a DBPR mold license, and you can confirm it in the same lookup. If a company can't give you a license number on request, that's a reason to keep calling around.

## What should a full-service fire restoration company handle?

A full-service fire restoration company handles six things: emergency board-up, water extraction from suppression, soot and smoke residue cleaning, odor control, contents cleaning or pack-out, and coordination with your insurance adjuster.

Board-up and tarping happen first, usually within hours, to secure broken windows and a compromised roof against weather and break-ins. Next comes extracting any water left behind from the fire department's hoses, since Florida humidity turns that into a mold problem fast if it's ignored. Soot cleaning follows, and it's not just surface wiping: soot is acidic and etches metal and glass within days, so sequencing matters. Odor control, whether thermal fogging, ozone, or hydroxyl treatment, usually happens after the structure is dry, not before. ## What should you ask about insurance billing before you hire?

Ask whether the company bills your insurer directly or expects you to pay and submit for reimbursement, and get that answer in writing before work starts.

Also ask: Do they provide a written scope of work with line-item pricing before starting? Will they document damage with photos and moisture readings for the claim file? Have they worked with your specific carrier before? A company that's vague on any of these is harder to work with mid-claim, not easier. If a claim gets disputed, Florida's [Division of Consumer Services](https://myfloridacfo.com/division/consumers), under the Chief Financial Officer's office, handles insurance complaints and can help you understand your rights.

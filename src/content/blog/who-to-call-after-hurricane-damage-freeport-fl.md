## Who do you call first after hurricane damage in Freeport?

Call 911 or your utility company first only if someone is in danger right now. If the house is stable and everyone is safe, your first call is your insurance company, not a contractor.

That order matters more than it sounds like it should. Florida's Department of Financial Services has pushed this guidance hard after every major storm: notify your insurer before you sign anything with a contractor, because once you sign certain repair agreements, you may have handed over some control of your own claim. You don't need to do that to get help fast.

After the insurer, your next call is a storm restoration crew that can tarp the roof and start pulling water out of the structure, usually the same day. Permanent repairs like a new roof or tree removal come after that, once the house is dry and the damage is on record.

## When do you call 911 or the utility company instead?

Call 911 or the power company immediately if there's a downed power line, a gas odor, or structural damage that makes part of the house unsafe to be in. Those calls always come before insurance or restoration.

Downed lines near standing water are a real electrocution risk in a storm-soaked yard, and a cracked gas line is not something to wait out. If a tree has come through a load-bearing wall or the roof structure looks like it's sagging, stay out of that section of the house until the line is cut and the structure is checked. Everything else on this list can wait until the scene is physically safe.

## Why call your insurer before signing with any contractor?

Calling your insurer first protects your claim, because Florida has specific rules around who you authorize to deal directly with your insurance company on your behalf.

That authorization is called an assignment of benefits, or AOB, and Florida's Department of Financial Services warns homeowners to read any contract carefully before signing one in a storm's aftermath, since an AOB can sign away your right to negotiate your own claim. You do not need to sign an AOB to get emergency tarping or water extraction started. A reputable crew will document the damage, give you a written scope, and let your insurer and your contractor communicate without you losing control of the claim.

Once the claim is open, your insurer will usually tell you to mitigate further damage immediately rather than wait for an adjuster visit, which is exactly what a restoration crew is for.

## What does a 24/7 restoration crew do when they arrive?

A storm restoration crew's job in the first hours is to stop the damage from getting worse: tarping the roof, boarding broken windows, and extracting standing water before it soaks into subfloor and drywall.

That means plywood over blown-out windows, poly sheeting and tarps lashed down over a torn roof section, and extraction equipment running on any wet flooring or carpet pad within the first day. Photos and moisture readings get logged at every stage, which becomes the documentation your adjuster will want to see. ## How do you verify a Florida contractor's license before work starts?

You can verify any Florida contractor's license for free through the state's online license lookup before they start work on your home.

After a hurricane, out-of-town crews often show up canvassing damaged neighborhoods, and Florida's licensing board runs a dedicated storm recovery verification tool specifically because of that pattern. Look up the business name or license number before signing anything, and be cautious of anyone who can't produce one on request or who pressures you to sign a contract on the spot.

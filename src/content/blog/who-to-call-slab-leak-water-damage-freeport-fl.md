## What Are the Signs of a Slab Leak?

A slab leak usually shows up as a warm or damp patch on the floor, a section of flooring that buckles or separates from the slab, or baseboards and the bottom few inches of drywall that feel soft or discolored. These are symptoms of water traveling up through or along the concrete, not of a problem you can see directly.

Other common signs: a water meter that keeps spinning even with every fixture off, a drop in water pressure, or the sound of running water when nothing is turned on. In Freeport's humid climate, that trapped moisture under carpet pad, vinyl plank, or tile doesn't just sit there. It wicks sideways into baseboards and the lower few inches of drywall, which is why the damage often looks worse a week in than it did on day one.

## Who Finds and Fixes a Slab Leak in Freeport?

A licensed plumber is who finds and repairs a slab leak, not a water damage restoration company. Locating a leak under a concrete slab requires specialized equipment (acoustic listening devices, thermal imaging, sometimes a controlled access point through the slab itself) and the repair itself is plumbing work that requires a plumbing license to perform legally in Florida.

Veterans does not do plumbing and does not locate or repair slab leaks. For that part of the job, call a licensed plumber directly. Once the leak is found and the water is shut off or rerouted, that's the point where a water damage restoration crew comes in to deal with what the leak left behind.

## Who Dries the Slab and Flooring After the Leak Is Fixed?

Veterans Remediation & Restoration handles the water removal and drying once the plumber has stopped the source. That means pulling up affected flooring where needed, extracting standing water, and setting up air movers and dehumidifiers to dry the exposed slab, subfloor materials and any wet drywall or baseboard down to a safe moisture level.

Concrete is porous and holds moisture longer than people expect. A slab that looks dry to the touch can still be wet several inches down, which is why drying crews use moisture meters to track actual readings in the material rather than guessing from the surface. Baseboards and the lower section of drywall that wicked water usually need to come out rather than dry in place, since trapped moisture behind painted drywall rarely evaporates on its own. ## What About Mold Risk and Insurance Coverage?

Mold can start growing on wet drywall and flooring within 24 to 48 hours, per the [EPA's guidance on mold cleanup](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-cleanup-your-home), and Walton County's humidity doesn't slow that clock down. Any wet material under flooring or inside a wall cavity that isn't fully dried within a couple of days becomes a mold risk, which is why the drying step isn't optional even after the leak itself is fixed. If mold has already started by the time a drying crew arrives, that part of the job shifts to licensed mold remediation rather than straight drying.

On insurance, most Florida homeowners policies cover the sudden water damage caused by a slab leak (the drying and the flooring that has to come out) but typically do NOT cover the plumbing repair itself, since that's considered a mechanical failure rather than water damage. Whether your insurer approves tear-out versus a more limited repair often comes down to documentation: moisture readings, photos, and a written scope from whoever did the drying work. The [Florida Department of Financial Services' consumer insurance resources](https://www.myfloridacfo.com/division/consumers) are a reasonable starting point if you want to understand your policy language before you file.

## What Happens When You Call?

When you call about a slab leak, expect to be asked how long the area has been wet, which rooms are affected, and whether the plumber has already located or stopped the leak. If the leak hasn't been found yet, get that call made first, since drying can't really start in earnest until the water source is confirmed stopped.

Have your insurance information ready if you plan to file a claim, and take photos of the affected flooring and baseboards before anything is moved or removed. A drying crew on-site will typically map out the wet area with a moisture meter, remove flooring or baseboard that's too saturated to dry in place, and set equipment to bring the slab and remaining materials back down to normal moisture levels over the following days.

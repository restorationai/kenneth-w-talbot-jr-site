## What Should You Ask Before Hiring Any of Them?

Ask four things before you sign anything: are they licensed and insured in Florida, will they document moisture readings with photos for your insurance claim, do they give you a written scope of work before starting, and who actually shows up (an employee or a subcontractor). A company that hesitates on any of these is worth a second look elsewhere.

Water that's been sitting for more than 24 to 48 hours starts to create conditions for mold growth on wet materials, according to the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home). That's the practical reason response speed matters more in this trade than in almost any other home service call. Our [burst pipe emergency checklist](/blog/burst-pipe-emergency-checklist/) covers what to do in the first hour while you're deciding who to call.

Insurance is the other piece people get wrong. Most homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage like a burst pipe, but they typically exclude gradual leaks and require photo documentation to support the claim. Our post on [whether homeowners insurance covers water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/) breaks down what's usually covered and what isn't, and the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/settling-insurance-claims-after-a-disaster) has a good overview of how claims typically get settled after a loss.

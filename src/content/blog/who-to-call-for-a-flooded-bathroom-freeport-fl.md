## Do You Need a Plumber or a Water Damage Company First?

Call whichever professional matches the problem in front of you. A plumber stops water that is still flowing. A water damage restoration crew removes the water already on the floor and dries out what it soaked into. Most flooded bathrooms end up needing both, just in that order.

A cracked supply line, a failed wax ring under the toilet, or a burst flex hose on the sink all need a plumber to physically fix the part. A restoration crew does not do plumbing repair. Their job starts once the source is stopped: pulling standing water, running [emergency water removal](/services/emergency-water-removal/) equipment, and drying the structure so the leak doesn't turn into a second, slower problem.

If the leak is small and you can stop it yourself with a shutoff valve, you may only need the restoration side. If it's an active pipe failure, the plumber comes first, even if that means a short delay before drying starts.

## How Do You Shut Off the Water Yourself?

Most bathroom fixtures have a small oval shutoff valve, called an angle stop, right at the wall behind the toilet or under the sink. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

If the angle stop is corroded and won't turn, or you can't find one, shut off the home's main water valve instead, usually near the water meter or where the line enters the house. For a washing machine, the two hose valves are typically on the wall behind the machine. ## Is the Water Clean, Gray, or Sewage?

Water from a supply line, a tank refill, or a fixture that hasn't touched the drain side is Category 1, clean water. A toilet bowl overflow is Category 2 if it's just urine, and Category 3, black water, if it contains feces or backs up from anywhere past the trap.

This distinction matters for how the job gets handled under the [ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/). Category 3 water calls for PPE, antimicrobial treatment, and often removing porous materials like baseboard and wet drywall rather than trying to dry them in place. Don't mop up a sewage backup yourself; let a crew with the right protective gear handle it.

## Does a Washing Machine Overflow Need the Same Steps?

Yes. A washing machine overflow follows the same triage as a bathroom leak. Shut off the two supply valves behind the machine first.

Then consider the source: overflow from the fill cycle is usually clean, Category 1 water, while a backed-up or overflowing drain cycle carries detergent, lint, and soil and is treated as Category 2. Either way, water that reached the subfloor under laundry room flooring needs the same extraction and drying equipment a bathroom leak would.

## When Does a Small Bathroom Leak Need Drying Equipment?

A bathroom leak needs air movers and a dehumidifier whenever water has soaked under the flooring or into the subfloor, not just sitting on top of the tile. Vinyl plank flooring hides this well.

Vinyl plank can look completely dry on the surface while the underlayment and subfloor underneath stay saturated for days. A moisture meter pressed through the seams tells the real story. Left wet, that trapped moisture is enough for mold to start within 24 to 48 hours on an organic subfloor, per [EPA guidance on mold cleanup](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-cleanup-your-home). That's also the window covered in more depth in [how fast water damage mold grows and how to stop it](/blog/water-damage-mold/). If a leak has been running longer than an hour, or you can still feel softness in the floor the next morning, that's a sign it needs equipment, not just a mop and a fan.

## What Happens When You Call a Restoration Crew?

A restoration crew maps the wet areas with a moisture meter, extracts standing water, and sets drying equipment before the first visit is over. They also document the damage for your insurance file.

On arrival, expect a quick walkthrough to check how far the water traveled (behind the vanity, under the toilet flange, into the hallway), extraction with a wet vac or truck-mounted unit, and air movers plus a dehumidifier placed to dry the floor, subfloor, and lower wall cavity over the following days. If the source was Category 3, containment and antimicrobial treatment happen before drying equipment goes in. Most homeowners' policies cover sudden water damage like this, though the rules differ for gradual leaks; see [does homeowners insurance cover water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/) for how that typically works.

A flooded bathroom rarely needs a demolition crew, but it does need someone checking moisture under the surface, not just mopping what's visible.

The order you make calls in, and what you document before cleanup starts, decides how smoothly the claim and the next booking both go.

## Who do you call first, a plumber or a water damage crew?

If water is actively running from a burst supply line, a failed washing machine hose, or an overflowing toilet, shut off the water at the fixture valve or the unit's main first. Then call a water damage crew to start extraction and drying while you separately line up a licensed plumber for the actual repair.

Those are two different jobs. A plumber fixes the pipe, valve, or water heater that failed. A restoration crew pulls the standing water, pulls wet baseboard or carpet pad that won't dry in place, and runs air movers and a dehumidifier to get the structure back to a normal moisture reading. Doing only one of those leaves either an active leak or a wet wall cavity that keeps drying out the room and feeding mold.

For an active structural leak, read the step-by-step in [what to do in the first 24 hours after water damage](/blog/what-to-do-first-24-hours-water-damage/) while you're waiting on a callback.

## How do you get a drying crew in without losing the next booking?

Most Class 1-2 water losses (a single room, with limited water absorbed into only a few materials, caught same day) dry out in three to five days with air movers and a dehumidifier running continuously. That timeline is the number to compare against your next check-in.

Tell the crew your next arrival date up front. A restoration team that works vacation rentals knows to stage equipment so it's out of walkways, note which rooms are usable versus off-limits, and pull equipment the moment moisture readings hit dry standard rather than running it a day longer than necessary out of caution. If the unit won't be dry before the next guest, that's a conversation to have with your booking platform or guest directly, not something to discover the day of check-in.

For a condo or multi-unit building where the leak came from a neighboring unit, the same triage applies but with an added step: documenting which unit the water originated from matters for whichever insurer ends up paying. ## What do you need to document for the insurance claim while guests are still in the unit?

Photograph the damage before anything gets moved or cleaned, including wide shots of the room and close-ups of the water source, then keep every receipt tied to the loss. A short-term rental policy and a standard homeowners policy don't cover the same things, so get the specifics of your actual policy confirmed before you assume anything is covered.

Most landlord and short-term rental policies, like standard homeowners policies, cover sudden accidental water damage (a pipe that burst, a water heater that failed) but not gradual leaks or flood from rising water, which needs separate flood coverage. A guest incident report, a timestamped moisture reading, and photos from the day of the leak all strengthen the file. The [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/settling-insurance-claims-after-a-disaster) has a general rundown of how disaster claims get settled if you want the plain-language version before your adjuster calls.

Homeowners facing a similar question off a different cause can also check [does homeowners insurance cover water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/) for how the sudden-versus-gradual line gets drawn.

## What about leaks you don't find until the cleaner shows up?

AC condensate lines and water heaters fail quietly in rentals that sit closed between bookings, and nobody notices until a cleaner opens the door to a soaked carpet or a musty smell. A clogged condensate line can drip for days behind a closet wall before it shows on the ceiling below.

That quiet-failure pattern is also how mold gets a head start in closed-up units. Mold can begin growing on wet drywall or carpet pad in as little as 24 to 48 hours, according to the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-cleanup-your-home), and a rental that sits shut with the AC off between guests holds moisture longer than an occupied home does. If your cleaner reports a damp smell or visible staining, treat it as a leak investigation, not just a cleaning note. A [water heater flood cleanup](/services/water-heater-flood-cleanup/) call and a [mold remediation](/services/mold-remediation/) inspection are two different services worth knowing apart, since catching the leak early can mean you only need the first one.

## What happens when you call?

Expect a few quick questions: the property address, what's wet and how long it's been that way, and whether the water is still running. Have your gate or lockbox code ready since you likely won't be on-site yourself.

On arrival, the crew checks the water source, takes moisture readings on walls and flooring to map how far the water traveled, and sets extraction and drying equipment. They'll also photograph the damage for your insurance file before anything gets removed. Ask for a written scope of the work before it starts, since every loss is a little different and a scope in writing protects both the claim and the calendar.

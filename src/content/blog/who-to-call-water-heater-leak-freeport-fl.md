Most homes around Freeport, including the slab-on-grade construction common in neighborhoods like Hammock Bay, keep the water heater in a garage or a small interior utility closet with no floor drain nearby. When the tank splits or a fitting fails, water has nowhere to go but across the slab and under the baseboards into the laundry room, hallway, or garage drywall. By the time most homeowners notice the puddle, water has already been sitting for hours.

## Should I call the plumber or the restoration company first?

Call both at roughly the same time. They handle different parts of the same emergency and don't need to happen in sequence. Shut off the water supply to the heater first, the cold-water line has a dedicated shutoff valve, usually on top of the tank or on the wall nearby, then make both calls.

If the heater is gas-fired, turn the gas control knob to "off" before you shut off the water, and don't try to relight anything yourself. The plumber inspects the tank, the fittings, the T&P valve, and the gas or electrical hookup, then repairs or replaces the unit. Veterans handles the standing water, pulls wet flooring and baseboard where needed, and sets up drying equipment so the moisture doesn't turn into a mold problem while the new heater gets installed. Neither company can do the other's job, and waiting on one before calling the other just gives the water more time to spread.

## When should I call 911 or the gas company instead?

Call 911 or your gas utility immediately, before either contractor, if you smell gas, hear hissing near the heater, or see water pooling near an electrical outlet or panel.

A water heater in a garage often sits close to the electrical panel or a dryer outlet. Standing water near live electrical components is a shock hazard, not a cleanup job. If there's any question about gas or electrical safety, get the hazard cleared by the utility or emergency responders first. Veterans can start extraction once the scene is confirmed safe, but technicians won't enter a space with an active gas leak or exposed live wiring.

## Will homeowners insurance cover the water heater leak?

Most homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage, like a tank that splits without warning, but typically exclude the cost of replacing the heater itself if the failure is tied to age or wear and tear.

Coverage depends on your specific policy and the cause of the failure, so this isn't a guarantee either way, check with your carrier. According to the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance), standard homeowners policies generally cover sudden and accidental water damage but exclude damage from gradual leaks or long-term neglect. If the heater had been dripping slowly for weeks before it failed, an adjuster may treat that differently than a tank that ruptured overnight. Keeping the shutoff time, photos, and the plumber's diagnosis on hand gives the claim the best chance.
